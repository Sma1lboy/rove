The folded task rail now shows exactly the projects the expanded tree shows. Folded and unfolded were deriving their sections from two different places: the tree built them with `buildTreeRows`, which hides a project you have closed down to nothing and files a repo's tasks under one header however the store interleaved them, while the rail walked the raw task list and started a new section whenever the key changed between neighbouring rows. Four things fell out of that. A repo closed down to its main checkout with the last tab shut kept a divider in the fold after the tree had dropped it. One project could fold to two dividers, which is the ordinary case rather than an exotic one, because `tasks.json` orders by creation and two repos worked on in turn arrive interleaved. A scratch session sat wherever the store had put it instead of on the bench at the top. And a task whose deletion was already in flight still held a row. Selection, grouping and order are now one shared answer that both surfaces read, so the fold cannot invent a list of its own; a section's letter also comes from the header the tree prints, which means two repos with the same basename fold to different letters (`work/api` and `oss/api` give `w──` and `o──`, not `a──` twice) instead of reading as one repo.
