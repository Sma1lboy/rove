On a phone, the web client's text is one step larger: lists and hints 14px, body text 16px, session titles 17px, session rows 52px tall with larger status marks, round buttons 44px with 22px icons, and the terminal's font 14px (45 columns on a 390px screen). The controls under a session — attach, the message field or hold-to-talk, send, and the key row — and the view switch in the session's top bar are now glass buttons in the style of iOS: see-through, blurred, with a lit top edge. Back and close are round glass buttons with only a chevron or ×, at the top left of every phone page, Settings and dialog, with the page's title beside them. The session list has a floating bar the list scrolls under: a gear for Settings at the bottom left and a solid accent New task button of the same size at the bottom right, in place of the New task row at the top. The key row shows six keys a thumb wide (esc, ⇧tab, ^C, ↑, ↓, ⏎) and keeps Ctrl, tab, ← and → behind ⋯. Under a session, attach, the message field, the mic and Send share one capsule on the key row's edges, and Send turns solid accent when there is something to send; no mic is shown where the browser cannot record. The list's wordmark is its large title and the inbox is a tray button with a count badge. Settings is a grouped list with icon tiles; its rows keep the control on the right and choices open a sheet from the bottom. Dialogs sit low, above the keyboard, with two equal buttons. On New session the chips span the card. They turn solid when the system asks for less transparency. The desktop web and the terminal app are unchanged.
